## The short version

A printing press makes more than a person can watch. I built a camera system
that checks the print as it comes off the line and flags the bad ones, so
problems get caught on the press and not by the customer.

## The problem

Checking was done by hand, on samples. Someone pulled a few sheets, looked at
them, and decided whether the whole run was fine. Anything that went wrong
between samples got through.

Three things made it hard to automate. The same flaw looks obvious on one
material and nearly invisible on another. The check has to keep up with the
press. And false alarms cost you twice: they waste the operator's time now, and
after enough of them people stop trusting the alarm.

## How it works

1. **Even out the picture.** Lighting and contrast are corrected first, so the
   system is looking at the print and not at how bright the material is.
2. **Find the flaws.** A model trained on examples from every material marks
   what looks wrong. One model for all of them, so there's one thing to
   maintain and not a family of them.
3. **Group the repeats.** The same fault turning up again and again is grouped
   as one problem, not four hundred separate alarms.

## What changed

- Detection went from **69 to 95** on the standard score for this kind of
  model (it's called mAP, and it's out of 100).
- Time spent on manual checks fell by **40%**.
- Recurring faults were identified **25%** more accurately, across more than
  700 sheets.
- It went into live use with real-time alerts, and tracing a fault back to its
  source got **35%** better.

## What I'd do next

Make the grouping part of the live system, so the press can say *this is the
same fault as an hour ago* and not raise a fresh alarm each time.

And add a standing check that today's prints still look like the ones the
model learned from, with a warning when they don't.
