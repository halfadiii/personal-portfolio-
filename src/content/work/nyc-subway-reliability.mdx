## The short version

The subway tells you when a train is coming. It never tells you when it
actually showed up. I built something that works that out, and then uses it to
measure how long people really wait on the platform.

## The problem

The MTA's live data is a forecast. For every train it lists the stops still
ahead and when it expects to reach them, and it rewrites that list every few
seconds. When a train arrives, nothing gets written down. Its row just
disappears.

So you can't answer the obvious questions. How long will I wait? Is this line
worse at night? You'd need a record of arrivals, and there isn't one.

## How it works

1. **Save a copy of the forecast every 30 seconds.** All eight of the MTA's
   feeds, each copy kept exactly as it came.
2. **Spot what disappeared.** If a train was due at a stop and then it's gone
   from the list, it arrived. The last time it was predicted is when.
3. **Don't get fooled.** A train also disappears when it's cancelled. If it
   vanished while it was still more than two minutes away, it didn't arrive,
   so it doesn't count.
4. **Measure the wait.** Arrivals give you the gaps between trains, and the
   gaps give you how long a rider actually waits.

Each run only works through what's new since the last one, and a test checks
that this gives exactly the same answer as redoing everything from scratch.

## Why not just average the gaps

Because trains bunch. Six trains an hour sounds like one every ten minutes. But
if they come two minutes apart and then eighteen, most people turn up during
the eighteen. The average gap is still ten. The average wait isn't. That extra
is called excess wait, and it's the number that matches what a bad commute
feels like.

## What it found

In its longest run so far, 150 minutes overnight, it read 1.6 million
predictions and worked out 9,344 arrivals. The worst spot was the A train at
59 St–Columbus Circle: trains about 20 minutes apart, and riders waiting nearly
9 minutes longer than they would have if those trains were evenly spaced.

I also asked whether rain makes the wait worse. On eleven years of the MTA's
own figures it doesn't, not in any way I can measure. That chart is at the
bottom of this page.
